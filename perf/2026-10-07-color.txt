Cutline colour benchmark: what one colour tool costs on a 1080p frame (scripts\bench.bat --color)
Machine: AMD Radeon AI PRO R9700, 32 hardware threads, Windows 11, Release build (MSVC /O2), float source, 8-bit output.
Each case is one clip with one effect; "software" is the whole frame composed on the CPU (decode excluded: the source is a held
picture), "GPU" is the whole frame on the card including the upload and the read-back, "on the card" is the shaders alone.
The first case has no effect at all and is the floor every other case sits on.

BEFORE (one thread; software compositor as it was at the start of the day, 20 frames per case):
  basic color (grade)   software     24.4 ms   GPU    4.4 ms (on the card 0.33)
  LUT 17^3              software     55.8 ms   GPU: not on the card (the lut effect)
  LUT 33^3              software     60.6 ms   GPU: not on the card (the lut effect)
  LUT 65^3              software     62.6 ms   GPU: not on the card (the lut effect)
  color wheels          software     67.5 ms   GPU: not on the card (the color_wheels effect)
  curves                software     39.6 ms   GPU: not on the card (the curves effect)
  hue curves            software    141.8 ms   GPU: not on the card (the hue_curves effect)
  color adjust          software     44.0 ms   GPU: not on the card (the color_adjust effect)
  HSL secondary         software     80.8 ms   GPU: not on the card (the hsl_secondary effect)
  channel mixer         software     22.7 ms   GPU: not on the card (the channel_mixer effect)
  tint                  software     25.5 ms   GPU: not on the card (the tint effect)
  black and white       software     26.9 ms   GPU: not on the card (the black_and_white effect)

What this does and does not say. The software figures depend on the number of cores (32 threads here; a 4-core machine will
gain a third of this). The floor of about 12 ms is the rest of the frame (picture copy, compositing, float to 8-bit), not
colour work, and is not yet optimised. The GPU wall time is dominated by uploading the picture and reading the result back
(about 3.3 ms together), not by the shaders. One adapter, one vendor.
